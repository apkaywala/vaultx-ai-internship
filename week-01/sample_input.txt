Artificial intelligence is transforming how software gets built. Just five years ago, writing a working prototype required weeks of manual coding, testing, and debugging. Today, developers can describe what they want in plain language and get functional code in minutes. This shift is not just about speed — it is changing who gets to build software in the first place. People with no formal programming background are now shipping real tools, automating their own workflows, and solving problems that would have required hiring a developer.

That said, this shift brings real concerns. Code generated quickly is not always secure, efficient, or maintainable, and relying too heavily on AI-generated code without understanding it can create hidden risks down the line. There is also a growing skills gap: junior developers who lean entirely on AI tools may never build the deep debugging instincts that come from struggling through problems manually. The teams that will benefit most are the ones that treat AI as a powerful assistant, not a replacement for understanding the fundamentals.
